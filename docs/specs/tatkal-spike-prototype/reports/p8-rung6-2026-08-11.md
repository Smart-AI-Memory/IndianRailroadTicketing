# P8 — rung 6 bot classifier (2026-08-11)

**Equal-effort note (R7):** the classifier is a two-feature interpretable
rule (arrival offset, min inter-request gap) with thresholds tuned by
deterministic grid search on labelled runs — the same budget class as the
classical rungs' one-or-two tuned constants. Trained on the SNIPER family
only (seeds 101-103): o_thr=0.06s, g_thr=0.2s.
Burst and mimic families are HELD OUT (R7.1 circularity guard).

## The drain-speed finding

At rung 4's fast drain (sell-out ~100 ms) the classifier is structurally
blind: everyone present is 'early' (offset flags the whole contending
population; two-priority degenerates to FIFO) and no user has a second
request yet, so cadence is silent. F is unchanged (column 4). Rung 6
recovers fairness only because the adaptive stack's slow drain keeps the
contest open long enough for honest users to arrive and for cadence to
exist. This is the spec's standing note made empirical: **the race is
over before the population differentiates** — timing-only classification
cannot fix a sub-second scarcity race; only mechanisms that lengthen the
contest (waiting room + paced drain) give classification anything to
work with. Belongs in the write-up's mechanism-design discussion.

**The mimic breach is the honest headline of P8.** Against human-mimicking
automation the classifier cannot find the bots (TPR ~0.25) but still pays
its false-positive cost (~14% of honest users deprioritized, skewed toward
fast-cadence/early humans) — a small net fairness LOSS, just over the
pre-registered 5% bound. Under the ratified improvement rule, rung 6 may
claim a fairness improvement against naive and burst automation only; the
claim does not survive the held-out mimic family. This is exactly the
outcome the R7.1 circularity guard exists to force into the open, and it
lands where the spec's expected-result hypothesis pointed: ML earns its
keep in bot detection, on fairness, and only against automation that
behaves unlike humans.

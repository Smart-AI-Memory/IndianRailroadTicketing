## DC6 grading (as registered — misses reported, never adjusted)

Knee (throughput argmax): C = 2 on both engines. Criteria evaluated
at 8× knee (C = 16):

| criterion | registered | MariaDB | Postgres (comparator) | verdict |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| (a) identifiable knee | exists | C = 2 | C = 2 | **pass** |
| (b) p50 at 8× knee ≤ 2× knee p50 | ≤ 2.0 | **8.45** | 3.46 | **FAIL** |
| (c) p99 at 8× knee ≥ 10× knee p99 | ≥ 10 | **5.64** | 50.13 | **FAIL** |

**Verdict: engine-independence of the fitted server shape is
FALSIFIED.** The MariaDB curve is not the Postgres curve:

- **Postgres degrades tail-first**: p99 explodes 50× while p50 grows
  3.5×; p99/p50 at C = 16 is ~25×. Throughput *collapses* past the
  knee — 31.6% of peak at C = 256.
- **MariaDB queues fairly**: p50 and p99 grow together, linearly in
  C (p99 ≈ 1.2–1.4× p50 at every level — no tail separation at
  all), and throughput *holds* — 96.2% of peak at C = 256.

Same SQL, same harness, same machine, same load: two qualitatively
different contention signatures. "Flat median, exploding tail" is a
property of the Postgres lineage, not of hot-row contention as such.

## Criterion-calibration miss (reported, not adjusted)

DC6(b) is failed **by the Postgres anchor itself** (3.46 > 2). The
registered criteria encoded the synthesis slogan ("flat median,
exploding tail") rather than statistics computed from the Postgres
CSV — the proposer did not run the 2-second computation before
registration. Under the discipline this stands as a recorded miss:
the criteria are graded as registered, and the mis-calibration is
itself part of the result. Had (b) been calibrated to the Postgres
record (say ≤ 4–5×), the verdict would be unchanged — MariaDB fails
(b) at 8.45 and (c) by an order of magnitude in tail behaviour — so
the falsification does not hinge on the miscalibrated constant.

## What this means for v3 (consequences, not adjustments)

1. **R4.3 vindicated, upgraded in importance.** The three-variant
   bracketing (plateau/fitted/cliff) is no longer defensive
   hygiene — the two real engines measured so far land on *different
   variants* (MariaDB ≈ plateau with proportional latency; Postgres
   ≈ fitted). Every headline that is variant-dependent is now known
   to be engine-dependent in reality, not hypothetically.
2. **The simulator is not invalidated** — it is *scoped*. Fitted-
   variant results describe Postgres-like engines; plateau-variant
   results just acquired a real-world instance.
3. **Sharded control (report-only):** MariaDB C=64 sharded8 p99
   ≈ 24.7 ms vs hot ≈ 19.0 ms — sharding relieves nothing because
   there is no hot-key tail to relieve; consistent with the fair-
   queueing signature.
4. The synthesis's threat-to-validity #1 is updated by addendum
   (either-way obligation, D8) — the second anchor did not confirm
   the shape; it bounded the claim.
